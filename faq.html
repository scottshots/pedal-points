Getting started

What is Pedal Points?

Pedal Points is a free app that turns your bike rides into points. Start an errand ride when your bike replaces a car trip, or a leisure ride for fun. Your phone's GPS counts the miles, the app shows the gas money you saved, and you spend your points on rewards you choose for yourself, like a coffee or a dinner out.

Is Pedal Points free?

Yes. There's no account, no sign-up and nothing to buy. If you enjoy it, there's an optional "buy the maker a coffee" tip through PayPal on pedalpoints.app.

Does it work on Android and iPhone?

The Android app is in testing on Google Play right now. On any phone, including iPhone, you can use Pedal Points today in your web browser at pedalpoints.app/app and add it to your home screen.

How do I become an Android tester?

Join the tester group using the Google account your Android phone uses for Google Play. Then open the tester link, tap Become a tester, and install Pedal Points from Google Play. Keep it installed for 14 days. Google needs at least 12 testers before the app can go public.

Points and savings

How are points worked out?

Every mile earns 10 points, and errand miles count double. Bonuses are added per mile for freezing weather, heat, snow, rain, night riding and any bonus buttons you turn on. From December through March everything earns 1.5x, and you earn extra for climbing hills and for riding days in a row.

What's the difference between an errand ride and a leisure ride?

Errand rides replace a trip you'd otherwise drive, like going to the store or the post office, so they save gas and earn double points. Leisure rides are for fun, like a ride around the lake.

Does Pedal Points pay real money?

No. Pedal Points doesn't pay out money or gift cards, and it never pays for prizes. You buy your own rewards. Points and reward dollars are just a game. Cashing in is your own permission to treat yourself, paid for with the money you're saving on gas by riding instead of driving.

Doesn't food cost money too?

Yes, but less than you might think. A 10-mile ride on a regular bike burns roughly 400 to 500 calories, about what's in a banana and a slice of peanut butter toast, around $1 of food. On an e-bike you burn fewer calories, and recharging the battery for that ride costs only pennies. The app counts the gas you saved, not food. If you'd like to account for snacks, lower your reward boost a little.

My car gets great gas mileage, so my savings look small.

That's how it should work. Enter your own car's fuel economy and your local gas price in Settings, and the gas savings match your car. A hybrid saves less gas per mile than a pickup truck. Gas is only part of what driving costs, though. The IRS mileage rate, which also covers wear, tires, maintenance and depreciation, is about 76 cents a mile in 2026. If you'd like your rewards to count more than gas, raise your reward boost.

What about the electricity to charge my e-bike?

It's tiny. Most e-bikes use roughly 15 to 25 watt-hours per mile, so recharging after a 10-mile ride costs a few cents, even at California electric rates.

The real savings aren't just gas.

Agreed. Riding means fewer miles on your car, so it may last years longer, and you get the health benefits too. Those are hard to put a number on, so the app lets you choose: raise your reward boost to give yourself credit for whatever feels fair.

Using the app

Can I give myself extra points for wind, dirt roads or anything else?

Yes. Windy? is already one of your bonus buttons. In Settings, under bonus buttons, you can add your own, like Headwind? or Dirt road?, and choose how many bonus points per mile it's worth. Tap it during a ride to add the bonus.

I got in a car and forgot to end my ride. What do I do?

No problem. On the Stats tab under Recent rides, tap Edit on that ride. Lower the distance to what you really rode on the bike, and clear or lower the max speed. If the whole thing was a car trip, tap Delete this ride.

Is my location shared or tracked?

There are no accounts and no tracking. Your rides and points are stored only on your phone, and your route is never saved or sent anywhere. When you start a ride, the app sends your approximate location to a free weather service for the temperature, and the live map loads map pictures of your area. Nothing else leaves your phone.

Safety

Is riding right for everyone?

No, and that's okay. Riding isn't safe or practical for everyone, and injuries or health problems have costs no app can calculate. Ride when it works for you, drive when it doesn't, and never ride in unsafe weather, darkness or traffic just to earn points.
